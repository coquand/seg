\section{The situation}

In \texttt{seg/model} each construction is made on an arbitrary context and
its stability under substitution is then proved for that construction: $\El$
along a code map (\seg{GElC}), $\Ext(\alpha)$ and its fibration (Theorem 7,
\seg{GExtSub*}, \seg{GExtNat*}), the glued type and its classifier
(\seg{GUaOp6}, \seg{GUaOp8}), the side data of the composition of the
universe (\seg{GCompU2}, \seg{GCompU3}).  Each such proof compares two
constructions that agree only up to equations, and transports the
structures (compositions, fillers, equivalences, classifiers) along them.
Measured over the 88 files of the univalence development (about 9800
lines): 36\% is the mathematics proper, 39\% proves that substitution
commutes with a construction, 15\% transports structures along equations
of types, 8\% moves structures between universe levels.

\section{The idea}

A type former is defined \emph{once}, on a presheaf that classifies its
data, and every instance is obtained from it by substitution.  This is the
point of view of natural models (Awodey) and of universe categories
(Voevodsky), and it is how local universes (Lumsdaine--Warren) obtain
type formers that are strictly stable under substitution.

\paragraph{El.}  The universe $\U$ is a presheaf of codes, and $\El$ is one
type over $\U$, with small fibres $\El(F)$.  A family given by a code map
$c : \Gamma \to \U$ is $\El[c]$, the substitution of $\El$ along $c$.  Then
\[ \El[c][s] = \El[c \circ s] \]
is the composition law of substitution, proved once for all types, and not
a property of $\El$.  The fibration of $\El[c]$ is the fibration of $\El$
substituted along $c$; its stability is again the composition law, now for
fibrations, proved once.

\paragraph{Ext.}  The type $\Ext(\alpha)$ at a point $\rho$ of $\Gamma$
depends only on the data of $\alpha$ restricted to $\rho$ (we already use
this ``local-data form'').  Let $\LD$ be the presheaf of such local data:
at stage $I$, a fibrant type $A$ over $I$, a cofibration $\psi$, a fibrant
type $B$ over the restriction to $\psi$, a map $\alpha : B \to A$ and an
equivalence structure on it.  Define $\Ext_{\U}$ once as a type over $\LD$,
with its fibration (Theorem 7, once), and for data $\alpha$ over $\Gamma$
with local data map $\ell_\alpha : \Gamma \to \LD$,
\[ \Ext(\alpha) := \Ext_{\U}[\ell_\alpha]. \]
Stability under $s : \Delta \to \Gamma$ then reduces to two facts:
\[ \Ext_{\U}[\ell_\alpha][s] = \Ext_{\U}[\ell_\alpha \circ s]
   \qquad\text{and}\qquad \ell_{\alpha[s]} = \ell_\alpha \circ s . \]
The first is the composition law, once for all types.  The second is one
equation between data (the restriction of the data of $\alpha[s]$ to a
point is the restriction of the data of $\alpha$ to its image), the only
thing left to prove for $\Ext$.  The same applies to the glued type, to
the data of the univalence operation (already a presheaf of data,
\seg{GUnivEq.UaU.Q4}), and to the side data of the composition of $\U$.

\paragraph{Levels.}  $\U$ is a presheaf at level $a{+}1$ whose types have
fibres at level $a$.  The operation needed is substitution of a
\emph{small} type over a \emph{large} context along a map from a small
context.  Defined once, it takes the place of the present transfer
layers, which move each structure down a level separately.

\section{What has been checked}

On a copy of the model, without changing the type theory:
\begin{itemize}
\item \seg{GSmall}: small types over large contexts, maps into them, and
  substitution along these maps, with the composition law proved once for
  all small types (22 declarations).
\item \seg{GSmallEl}: $\El$ defined once as a small type over $\U$.  The
  present $\El$ along a code map (\seg{GElC.Fam}) is \emph{by definition}
  $\El$ substituted along it (checked by \texttt{refl}), and the present
  lemma \seg{elSub}, with its exact statement, is an instance of the
  general composition law.
\end{itemize}
Not yet checked: the same for fibrations (mechanical), and $\Ext$ over a
presheaf of local data, which needs a coding of $\LD$ at level $a{+}1$ as
for $\U$.

\section{What the presentation would look like}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The base: a category, cofibrations, an interval with connections.
\item A universe: a presheaf of codes $\U$ with $\El$ fibrant.
\item For each type former $F$: a presheaf $\LD_F$ classifying its data, a
  type $F_{\U}$ over $\LD_F$ with its structure (for $\Ext$: Theorem 7),
  and the equation $\ell_{\alpha[s]} = \ell_\alpha\circ s$.
\item Generic substitution laws, once: for types, for fibrations, for
  equivalence data.
\end{enumerate}
The results stay abstract (any base satisfying (1)); what disappears is the
proof, for each construction, that it commutes with substitution.

\begin{remark}
This is the semantic counterpart of Ehrhard's presentation of categories
with families compared with Dybjer's: there the lifted substitution is
taken primitive so that composite substitutions coincide by construction;
here each type former is taken primitive on its classifying presheaf so
that its instances coincide by construction, and only the equations of
data remain.
\end{remark}
